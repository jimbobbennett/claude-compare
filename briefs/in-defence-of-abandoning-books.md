## Key facts
- Term for quitting a book unfinished: "DNF" (did not finish); Goodreads hosts a crowdsourced "abandoned" shelf/tag that readers apply to such books (https://gwern.net/goodreads)
- Librarian Nancy Pearl formulated the "Rule of 50" live on air on KUOW-FM Seattle in response to a caller asking how many pages she owed a book before quitting guiltlessly (https://www.theglobeandmail.com/arts/books-and-media/nancy-pearls-rule-of-50-for-dropping-a-bad-book/article565170/)
- Rule of 50: under 50, read 50 pages before deciding; over 50, subtract your age from 100 to get your page allowance; at 100 you may judge a book by its cover (https://www.theglobeandmail.com/arts/books-and-media/nancy-pearls-rule-of-50-for-dropping-a-bad-book/article565170/)
- Mathematician Jordan Ellenberg proposed the "Hawking Index" in a 2014 Wall Street Journal blog post as a joke metric of how far readers get before quitting (https://bookriot.com/bestselling-books-people-dont-finish/)
- Hawking Index method: take page numbers of a Kindle title's five most-highlighted passages, average them, divide by total pages (https://gizmodo.com/this-summers-bestselling-book-that-everyone-starts-and-1600896088)
- Method is no longer reproducible as originally run: Amazon's public "Popular Highlights" are now visible only to purchasers (https://bookriot.com/bestselling-books-people-dont-finish/)
- Named for Stephen Hawking's *A Brief History of Time*, the standard example of a bestseller bought more than read (https://gizmodo.com/the-books-everyone-starts-and-no-one-finishes-accordin-1600866004)
- Goodreads reported roughly one in five books read by its members are abandoned (2013) (https://www.csmonitor.com/Arts-Culture/Books/chapter-and-verse/2013/0605/Why-do-we-abandon-books-halfway-through)
- Goodreads' most-abandoned classics list: *Catch-22*, *The Lord of the Rings*, *Ulysses*, *Moby-Dick*, *Atlas Shrugged* (https://judyleedunn.com/2018/10/16/goodreads-why-readers-abandon-books/)
- Gwern's 2019 scrape found the raw "abandoned" tag counts conflate popularity with abandonment probability; proportional estimates give a completely different ranking from absolute counts (https://gwern.net/goodreads)
- Self-reported tagging badly understates real abandonment: tagged abandonment rate averages ~0.13% of reads, i.e. 1 in 769 (https://gwern.net/goodreads)
- Selection bias in abandonment lists: titles with "trashy" reputations (e.g. *Fifty Shades of Grey*, *Outlander*) may be over-tagged because quitting them is socially advertisable (https://www.bustle.com/p/the-15-most-frequently-unfinished-reads-according-to-goodreads-popular-abandoned-books-shelf-9877579)
- Sunk-cost effect — greater tendency to continue an endeavour once money, effort or time is invested — documented by Arkes & Blumer, *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*, 1985 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0749597885900494)
- Arkes & Blumer attribute the effect to a desire not to appear wasteful (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0749597885900494)
- E-readers lower the friction of quitting: abandoning is a private act of closing a file rather than a visible reshelving (https://www.csmonitor.com/Arts-Culture/Books/chapter-and-verse/2013/0605/Why-do-we-abandon-books-halfway-through)
- Counter-frame: Taleb's "antilibrary" (term coined by Umberto Eco), which treats unread books as a research tool rather than a debt (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antilibrary)

- Psychology of not quitting — sunk cost, waste-aversion, completion norms learned young; guilt as reported phenomenon

## Terms to define
- DNF — "did not finish"; reader shorthand and shelf label for an abandoned book
- Hawking Index (HI) — averaged page position of a Kindle title's five most-highlighted passages, as a percentage of total pages
- Popular Highlights — Amazon Kindle feature aggregating the passages most highlighted by readers
- Sunk-cost effect — persisting with an endeavour because of unrecoverable prior investment of money, time or effort
- Winner's curse (as used in the Goodreads analysis) — mis-estimation of rates for low-count items, inflating extremes among rarely rated books
- Conditional probability of abandonment — chance a book is abandoned given that a reader started it, rather than raw count of abandonments
- Rule of 50 — Pearl's heuristic for how many pages to read before quitting without guilt
- Antilibrary — a collection of owned but unread books, treated as a research tool
- Tsundoku — Japanese term for acquiring books and letting them pile up unread
- Zeigarnik effect — psychological tendency to remember and be nagged by unfinished tasks more than completed ones
