## Key facts
- TSA is required by law to screen every bag transported on a commercial aircraft, whether carry-on or checked (https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/factsheets/checked-baggage-inspection-system-missoula-international-airport)
- TSA screens 100% of checked baggage with Explosives Detection Systems (EDS), computed-tomography scanners producing 3D images of contents (https://tsa.gov/press/factsheets/technology)
- EDS is used in conjunction with explosives trace detection (ETD) machines to identify and resolve threats in checked baggage (https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-11-740)
- A checked baggage inspection system (CBIS) is an automated conveyor network that sorts and tracks bags from ticket counter through screening to the aircraft loading point (https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/factsheets/checked-baggage-inspection-system-missoula-international-airport)
- Traditional baggage handling systems (BHS) route bags on fixed conveyor paths using mechanical diverters, pushers or pop-up devices, with sort decisions made at predefined points (https://alstefgroup.com/bhs-sorting-technologies-explained/)
- Newer sorting technologies include cross-belt sorters, tilt-tray sorters, destination-coded vehicles (DCV) and individual carrier systems (ICS) (https://alstefgroup.com/bhs-sorting-technologies-explained/)
- After sorting, the bag tag is read in the bag room and the bag is placed in a bag cart or Unit Load Device (ULD), then towed to the aircraft by bag tug (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baggage_handler)
- On narrow-body and some wide-body aircraft, loading is manual: ramp agents move bags from cart to beltloader and position them by hand inside cramped cargo compartments (https://www.osha.gov/etools/baggage-handling/manual-loading-unloading)
- Only the largest aircraft use cargo containers loaded by an automated system operated by the ramp agent (https://www.osha.gov/etools/baggage-handling/automated-loading-unloading)
- IATA Resolution 753 makes tracking at four mandatory points compulsory for member airlines, with data shared with partners (https://lyngsoesystems.com/logistics/articles/iata-resolution-753-tracking)
- The ten-digit bag tag number is mandatory in all cases when recording a Resolution 753 tracking point; a tracking point may be a physical scan or a system record (https://www.iata.org/contentassets/5316edd3aafb4866876e37883211cfc4/baggage_tracking_implementation_guide.pdf)
- Delta invested $50m in RFID at 344 stations; RFID scanners read radio-wave chips embedded in the bag tag (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/delta-leverages-rfid-introduces-innovative-baggage-tracking-process-300259988.html)
- Baggage undelivered after 21 days is legally considered lost under the Montreal Convention unless recovered and returned (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Convention)
- Bags not reunited with owners within four months are shipped to Unclaimed Baggage in Scottsboro, Alabama, which buys them as-is without inventorying contents; contents are cleaned, data-wiped and priced for resale (https://www.cnn.com/2024/11/30/travel/unclaimed-baggage-store-alabama-cec, https://www.cbsnews.com/news/unclaimed-baggage-lost-luggage-store-scottsboro-alabama/)

## Figures
- Global mishandling rate 2024: 6.3 bags per 1,000 passengers, down from 6.9 in 2023 and 67% below 2007 (https://www.passengerterminaltoday.com/news/baggage/baggage-mishandling-rate-improves-by-26-in-europes-airports.html)
- 33.4 million mishandled bags in 2024, vs 33.8 million the previous year (https://www.passengerterminaltoday.com/news/baggage/baggage-mishandling-rate-improves-by-26-in-europes-airports.html)
- 22 million of those (over 66%) resolved and closed in SITA WorldTracer within 48 hours; 25% within 12 hours (https://www.passengerterminaltoday.com/news/baggage/baggage-mishandling-rate-improves-by-26-in-europes-airports.html)
- Delayed bags = 74% of all mishandled baggage (https://www.scribd.com/document/1012356401/1534-2025-SITA-Baggage-Insights-Report)
- Europe 2024 mishandling rate: 12.3 per 1,000 passengers, down from 15.7 in 2022 (https://www.passengerterminaltoday.com/news/baggage/baggage-mishandling-rate-improves-by-26-in-europes-airports.html)
- Worldwide air traffic up 8.2% in 2024 to a record 5.3 billion passengers (https://www.passengerterminaltoday.com/news/baggage/baggage-mishandling-rate-improves-by-26-in-europes-airports.html)
- US DOT, March 2024: 43.1 million bags handled, mishandled rate 0.52% (vs 0.58% March 2023) (https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/air-travel-consumer-report-march-2024-numbers)
- US DOT, July 2024: 45.6 million bags handled, mishandled rate 0.75% (https://www.bts.gov/newsroom/air-travel-consumer-report-july-2024-numbers)
- Montreal Convention liability ceiling: 1,519 SDR per passenger for lost, damaged or delayed baggage (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Convention)
- Claim deadlines: damage reported in writing within 7 days; delay within 21 days (https://myflyright.com/air-passenger-rights/montreal-convention/)
- Delta RFID: 99.9% tracking success rate; tracking events per bag rose from 4 to 16; over 2 billion RFID scan events annually; 16% improvement in mishandled rate (https://lyngsoesystems.com/logistics/supply-chain/cases/delta-air-lines, https://pro.delta.com/content/agency/gb/en/products-and-services/baggage/real---time-bag-tracking.html)
- Resolution 753 adoption: 44% of airports fully implemented, 41% in progress (https://www.travelextra.ie/four-point-resolution-753-baggage-tracking-installed-by-44pc-of-worlds-airports-iata/)
- Approximately 1,200 Advanced Imaging Technology units deployed at nearly 400 US airports (passenger-side context) (https://tsa.gov/press/factsheets/technology)
- Unclaimed Baggage founded 1970 with a borrowed $300 and a 1965 truck; store now occupies a city block; ~7,000 new items added daily; ~60% of merchandise is clothing (https://www.cnn.com/2024/11/30/travel/unclaimed-baggage-store-alabama-cec, https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/unclaimed-baggage-center)
- Missoula International Airport CBIS: two integrated EDS units (https://www.tsa.gov/news/press/factsheets/checked-baggage-inspection-system-missoula-international-airport)

## Terms to define
- BHS (baggage handling system) — the conveyor, sorter and control network moving checked bags through an airport
- CBIS (checked baggage inspection system) — the in-line screening portion of the BHS integrating EDS machines
- EDS (Explosives Detection System) — CT scanner producing 3D images of bag contents for automated threat detection
- ETD (explosives trace detection) — swab-based instrument detecting residue of explosive compounds
- ULD (Unit Load Device) — standardised machine-loadable container or pallet used in wide-body holds
- Licence plate number — the unique ten-digit bag tag identifier used for tracking and messaging
- IATA Resolution 753 — mandate for airlines to track bags at four points and share the data
- Tracking point — a recorded event where custody or status of a bag changes
- Cross-belt / tilt-tray sorter — high-speed carrier-based sortation devices that discharge bags sideways
- DCV (destination-coded vehicle) — individually routed cart carrying one bag through a high-speed track network
- Make-up area — the bag room zone where sorted bags are loaded into carts or ULDs by flight
- Beltloader — mobile conveyor raising bags from the apron to the aircraft hold door
- WorldTracer — SITA's industry-wide system for tracing and reconciling mishandled bags
- PIR (Property Irregularity Report) — the form filed at the airport that starts a lost/damaged bag claim
- SDR (Special Drawing Right) — IMF currency basket unit in which airline liability limits are set
- Mishandled baggage rate — bags delayed, damaged, pilfered or lost per 1,000 passengers (SITA) or as a % of enplaned bags (US DOT)
