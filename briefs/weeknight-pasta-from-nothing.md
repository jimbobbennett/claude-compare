## Key facts
- USDA FSIS advises using most cooked leftovers within 3 to 4 days (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/steps-keep-food-safe)
- Leftovers should be reheated to 165 °F before serving (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/steps-keep-food-safe)
- Perishable food left at room temperature more than 2 hours should be discarded — 1 hour if above 90 °F (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/steps-keep-food-safe)
- Cooling rule: food goes into shallow containers, straight into fridge or freezer, for rapid cooling (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/steps-keep-food-safe)
- Fridge-life rule of thumb differs sharply by ingredient: ~4 days cooked leftovers vs 1–2 days raw poultry and ground meat (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/refrigeration)
- Appliance thermometer recommended; fridge consistently 40 °F or below, freezer 0 °F (https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/refrigerator-thermometers-cold-facts-about-food-safety)
- Manufacturer method (Barilla): 8–12 quart pot filled three-quarters, for 1 lb pasta, water at a rolling boil before pasta goes in (https://www.barilla.com/en-us/help-with/pasta-kitchen-tips/how-to-cook-pasta)
- Barilla: salt the water at one tablespoon plus one teaspoon per gallon (https://www.barilla.com/en-us/help-with/pasta-kitchen-tips/how-to-cook-pasta)
- Barilla: adding oil to the water does not prevent sticking and can stop sauce clinging to the pasta (https://www.barilla.com/en-us/help-with/pasta-kitchen-tips/how-to-cook-pasta)
- Peer-reviewed physics of cheese sauce identifies starch concentration as the key variable in sauce stability, not cheese quantity (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00536)
- Below 1% starch relative to cheese mass, the sauce clumps — authors name this the "Mozzarella Phase" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00536)
- Same study finds a lower critical solution temperature: cheese-and-water sauces separate as temperature rises, so heat management matters more than vigour of stirring (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00536)
- Scrap-cooking context: US food waste estimated at 30–40% of the food supply (https://www.usda.gov/about-food/food-safety/food-loss-and-waste)
- EPA/USDA 2030 goal is a 50% cut in per-person food waste sent to landfill (https://www.usda.gov/about-food/food-safety/food-loss-and-waste/food-waste-faqs)

## Figures
- 3–4 days: fridge window for most cooked leftovers (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/steps-keep-food-safe)
- 165 °F: reheat temperature for leftovers (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/steps-keep-food-safe)
- 40 °F / 0 °F: target fridge and freezer temperatures (https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/refrigerator-thermometers-cold-facts-about-food-safety)
- 40–140 °F: danger zone in which pathogenic bacteria grow rapidly (https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/refrigeration)
- 1 tbsp + 1 tsp salt per gallon of water (https://www.barilla.com/en-us/help-with/pasta-kitchen-tips/how-to-cook-pasta)
- 8–12 quart pot, three-quarters full, per 1 lb pasta (https://www.barilla.com/en-us/help-with/pasta-kitchen-tips/how-to-cook-pasta)
- 2–3%: starch-to-cheese mass ratio for a stable, creamy cheese sauce (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00536)
- <1%: starch-to-cheese ratio at which system-wide clumping occurs (https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00536)
- 30–40%: share of US food supply wasted (https://www.usda.gov/about-food/food-safety/food-loss-and-waste)
- 133 billion pounds / $161 billion: USDA estimate of retail and consumer food loss, 2010 (https://www.usda.gov/about-food/food-safety/food-loss-and-waste)
- 218.9 lb per person: 2010 EPA baseline for food waste sent to disposal; 109.4 lb is the 2030 target (https://www.usda.gov/about-food/food-safety/food-loss-and-waste/food-waste-faqs)
- 335 lb (2018) and 349 lb (2019) of food waste per person leaving the human food supply chain (https://www.epa.gov/sustainable-management-food/united-states-2030-food-loss-and-waste-reduction-goal)

## Terms to define
- Al dente — pasta cooked firm to the bite, still resistant at the centre
- Emulsion — stable suspension of fat in water, here held together by dissolved starch
- Mantecare — Italian for the vigorous off-heat tossing of pasta with sauce and fat to bind it
- Pasta acqua / starchy pasta water — the cooking liquid carrying dissolved and leached starch, used as sauce thickener
- Gelatinisation — swelling and rupture of starch granules in hot water, the source of the water's thickening power
- Lower critical solution temperature — temperature above which a mixture separates rather than staying blended
- "Mozzarella Phase" — coined term for starch-poor cheese sauce that seizes into stringy clumps
- Soffritto — foundation of aromatics slowly sweated in fat at the start of a sauce
- Danger zone — 40–140 °F range in which food-poisoning bacteria multiply fastest
- Umami anchor — concentrated savoury ingredient (anchovy, tomato paste, miso, parmesan rind) standing in for long-cooked depth
