## Key facts
- Academy of American Poets' guide frames poem-reading as a mix of disposition and skill, with curiosity free of preconceptions as the starting attitude and questions as the technique (https://poets.org/text/how-read-poem-0)
- Same guide argues form is part of meaning — repetition and rhyme amplify or extend a word or idea — so observable features are a practical entry point for a non-specialist (https://poets.org/text/how-read-poem-0)
- Same guide frames the goal of careful reading as an interpretive question with more than one answer, not a single correct decoding (https://poets.org/text/how-read-poem-0)
- Edward Hirsch's Poetry Foundation essay treats the lyric poem as a direct address between an "I" and a "You," beginning and ending in silence (https://www.poetryfoundation.org/articles/69955/how-to-read-a-poem)
- Hirsch: poetry's medium is common language; precision restores it, and defamiliarisation comes from wrenching words out of habitual contexts (https://www.poetryfoundation.org/articles/69955/how-to-read-a-poem)
- Hirsch: metaphor and figurative language are the mechanism guiding interaction between poet and reader (https://www.poetryfoundation.org/articles/69955/how-to-read-a-poem)
- Hirsch's *How to Read a Poem: And Fall in Love with Poetry* (1999) was a national bestseller; reader demand for its terms appendix produced *A Poet's Glossary* (2014) (https://poets.org/edward-hirschs-poets-glossary)
- Poetry Foundation maintains a free online Glossary of Poetic Terms covering forms and techniques with linked example poems (https://www.poetryfoundation.org/learn/glossary-terms)
- Billy Collins's "Introduction to Poetry" (1988) dramatises the difference between experiencing a poem and interrogating it for a hidden meaning; hosted free by Poetry Foundation (https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/46712/introduction-to-poetry)
- The same poem is item 001 in Poetry 180, the Library of Congress poet laureate project of a poem a day for schools (https://www.loc.gov/programs/poetry-and-literature/poet-laureate/poet-laureate-projects/poetry-180/all-poems/item/poetry-180-001/introduction-to-poetry/)
- Poem-a-Day publishes new work by contemporary poets on weekdays and public-domain classics with short critical analysis on weekends — free, by email (https://poets.org/poem-day-surpasses-quarter-million-subscribers)
- Each weekday Poem-a-Day entry includes an audio recording of the poet reading their own work (https://poets.org/announcing-2025-guest-editors-poem-day-academy-american-poets-online-series-premiering-original)
- National Poetry Month was founded by the Academy of American Poets in April 1996, modelled on Black History Month and Women's History Month (https://poets.org/national-poetry-month/about-celebration)
- NEA added a poetry *listening* question to its national survey for the first time in 2022, formally recognising audio as a mode of poetry consumption (https://www.arts.gov/news/press-releases/2023/new-data-reveal-how-adults-participated-arts-during-covid-19)
- Pádraig Ó Tuama's *Poetry Unbound* podcast (On Being Studios), launched 2020, offers single-poem close readings for general listeners (https://canongate.co.uk/books/4042-poetry-unbound-50-poems-to-open-your-world/)

## Suggested sections
- **The barrier** — schoolroom hangover, hunt-the-meaning habit, Collins's "Introduction to Poetry" as the canonical statement of the problem
- **Attitude before technique** — curiosity, tolerance for not-knowing, multiple valid answers, rereading as default
- **Mechanics a novice actually needs** — read aloud, line break vs sentence, stanza, sound before sense, who is speaking
- **Free primary sources** — Poem-a-Day, Poetry Foundation glossary and archive, Poetry 180, poets.org guides
- **Audio and podcast route** — Poetry Unbound, poet-read recordings, NEA's 2022 recognition of listening as participation
- **What to read first** — anthologies vs single collections, contemporary vs canon, single-poet immersion
- **Building a habit** — one poem a day, notebook, National Poetry Month hooks, reading groups

## Terms to define
- **Lyric poem** — short poem voicing a single speaker's feeling or thought, as distinct from narrative or dramatic verse
- **Close reading** — line-by-line attention to word choice, sound and structure rather than biography or context
- **Form** — the poem's shape and rules (sonnet, villanelle, free verse); part of meaning, not decoration
- **Metre** — the repeating pattern of stressed and unstressed syllables that sets a line's rhythm
- **Enjambment** — a sentence running past the end of a line into the next, creating tension between line and syntax
- **Stanza** — a grouped block of lines, the poem's equivalent of a paragraph
- **Free verse** — poetry without regular metre or rhyme scheme
- **Speaker (persona)** — the voice inside the poem, not automatically the poet
- **Volta** — the turn or shift in argument or mood, classically in a sonnet's ninth line
- **Prosody** — the study of versification: metre, rhythm, sound patterning
- **Abecedarian** — poem whose lines or stanzas begin with successive letters of the alphabet (https://www.poetryfoundation.org/learn/glossary-terms)
- **Paraphrase** — restating a poem in plain prose; useful as a check, limited as an account of what the poem does
